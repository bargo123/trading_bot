## Chapter 1 Position Sizing

Position sizing determines how large a trade is relative to account equity.
A common approach is to risk a small fixed percentage of capital on each trade.
If the stop is farther away, the position must be smaller to keep dollar risk constant.
Traders often choose risk between one half percent and two percent of equity per idea.
Sizing mistakes are a frequent cause of account damage even when the entry idea is sound.

## Chapter 2 Stop Losses

A stop-loss is an exit designed to cut a losing position when price moves against you.
Stops do not guarantee fills at the intended price during gaps or fast markets.
Some authors prefer structure-based stops while others prefer volatility-based stops.
The shared goal is defining invalidation before entering the trade.

## Chapter 3 Mean Reversion Limits

Mean-reversion strategies can fail during strong trends and volatility regime changes.
Authors often disagree about whether fixed fractional risk or volatility targeting is superior.
When evidence is missing, do not invent backtest statistics or precise performance claims.
A careful reader should separate researched results from opinionated market commentary.
