## Chapter 1 Liquidity

Liquidity describes how easily size can be transacted without large price impact.
Thin markets increase slippage risk and can invalidate otherwise reasonable setups.
Auction market theory discusses how price discovers value through participation.
Understanding session liquidity helps explain why identical setups behave differently by time of day.

## Chapter 2 Author Disagreement

Some authors emphasize discretionary price action. Others emphasize systematic rules.
When authors disagree, present both views and the context rather than forcing consensus.
Disagreement is normal in trading literature because markets are adaptive and path dependent.
An assistant should cite each view and avoid inventing a false synthesis.

## Chapter 3 Psychology

Trading psychology covers impulse control, loss acceptance, and process discipline.
Overconfidence after a winning streak is a common failure mode.
Fear after a drawdown can cause undersizing and abandoned edges.
Process journals are often recommended to separate emotional noise from statistical feedback.
