The fatal flaw is deciding directly on the sealed holdout: setting thresholds (60% confidence, +10% expectancy, −30% trade count) and iterating until they pass converts a one-shot holdout into a tuning set, leaving nothing clean to confirm the winner, and the proposal pre-commits to no one-query limit, no ExperimentRegistry entry, and no pre-registration of criteria before unlocking. Its central claim is unmeasurable as written — "positive net expected value after modeled spread and slippage" inherits whatever cost assumptions are fed in, with no reconciliation to realized demo fills, and a "≥10%" expectancy delta is asserted with no sample size, confidence interval, or multiple-testing correction despite an obvious family-of-variants search. The comparison design is also confounded: allowing up to 30% fewer trades changes regime exposure, so improved expectancy with flat max drawdown can come purely from dodging an unfavorable stretch of the holdout window; require exposure-normalized, risk-adjusted stats computed across sub-periods (walk-forward), not single-window point estimates. Finally, "advance it" must route through every gate in bot/aegis/research/promote.py rather than this bespoke criterion, and rejection-vs-promotion outcomes must be recorded either way — the proposal is silent on both governance paths.
