The filter's parameters (20-bar window, 20th/80th percentiles, 10% threshold) are asserted a priori with no tuning, sensitivity analysis, or in-sample justification—three free knobs chosen by fiat is textbook garden-of-forking-paths overfitting risk even before touching the holdout. "Net expectancy increases by at least 10%" is ambiguous (per-trade vs. aggregate, vs. which baseline run) and a single-pass holdout verdict on noisy metrics like max drawdown and expectancy has no confidence interval, bootstrap, or significance test behind it—one lucky path decides the champion. There's no stated limit on repeated challenges against the same sealed holdout (multiple-testing leakage) and no verification that the proposed accept criteria match or satisfy every gate in `bot/aegis/research/promote.py`; "accept as improvement candidate" on self-defined criteria is not a governed promotion. Finally, since a regime filter mechanically deletes trades, the 80% trade-count floor and unchanged-drawdown clause need an explicit definition of comparison windows, or the rejection rule itself becomes unfalsifiable.
