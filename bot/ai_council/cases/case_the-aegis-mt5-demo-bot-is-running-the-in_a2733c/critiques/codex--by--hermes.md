The proposal never defines "validated-state median" precisely enough to be falsifiable — median over which window and which regime? Spread distributions drift by session and liquidity conditions, so a fixed 1.5× multiplier calibrated on past states is a tuned hyperparameter with obvious overfitting surface (why 1.5 and not 1.3?), and no multiple-comparison control is declared across candidate thresholds. The expectancy comparison is confounded by composition change: abstaining under high spread alters which trades survive into the "taken" population, so "expectancy improves without lowering expectancy" is not an apples-to-apples claim — it needs a counterfactual estimate of the skipped trades' outcomes net of costs, not just re-measurement of survivors. "Statistically lower drawdown" is unmeasurable as stated — no metric (CVaR? max DD? at what confidence), no minimum skip count or power analysis for a rule that will fire rarely, and no out-of-time confirmation beyond a single sealed holdout, all while ignoring that the runner already has spread and remaining-EV gates this rule would partially duplicate. Safety-wise it's compliant (registered, sealed, promote-nothing-by-default), but it should explicitly route through `bot/aegis/research/promote.py` gates rather than implying its own promotion criterion.
