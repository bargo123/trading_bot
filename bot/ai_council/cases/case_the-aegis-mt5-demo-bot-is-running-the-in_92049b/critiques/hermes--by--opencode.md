The decision rule contradicts its own scope: the proposal states "no code, config, or champion changes are proposed now," yet pre-commits to demoting a protective gate threshold based entirely on simulated shadow fills that never face real spread widening, partial fills, or lifecycle invalidation — proving abstention "miscalibrated" with idealized executions is circular and exactly what `promote.py`'s gates exist to block. The statistics are underspecified and overfit-prone: two weeks is one regime, conditioning cohorts on the gate's own abstentions guarantees selection bias, the executed-trade sample size is never stated (≥200 shadow observations alone gives no power for the difference-in-EV test), and the bootstrap target statistic is ambiguous while the hypothesis ("equal or better") doesn't match the one-sided decision rule. Unmeasurable as written: the shadow exit rule, entry timing validity of the vaguely-cited "cached" pipeline, and the definition of a "calibrated notch" are all undefined, and there's no fallback registered if <200 observations accrue. Minimum fixes: pre-register shadow exit/lifecycle/fill assumptions, report both cohort sizes with a power calculation, require replication across a second regime window, and route any threshold change through the full governed promotion path rather than a single-study trigger.
