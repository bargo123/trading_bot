Raising the gate by 20% while exempting the incumbent champion from meeting it is incoherent governance—you're holding challengers to a standard the champion may already fail, with no stated rule for what happens then. The acceptance criteria are unmeasurable as written: no cost model specified for "net loss," no named risk-adjusted metric or evaluation window, no minimum holdout sample size or significance test, so noise on a thin sealed set can pass or reject arbitrarily. There is also no protection against holdout reuse—repeatedly evaluating tweaked challengers against one sealed set until one clears is textbook overfitting-by-selection, and the 15% coverage tolerance looks like a hand-tuned fudge factor rather than a pre-registered bound. Finally, nothing here mentions registering the experiment in `ExperimentRegistry`, running the full `promote.py` gate suite via the sanctioned promotion scripts, or governing the fact that modifying the gate itself is an infrastructure change, not merely a challenger test.
