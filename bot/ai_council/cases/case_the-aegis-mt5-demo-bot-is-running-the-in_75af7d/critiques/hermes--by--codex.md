The retrospective is selection-biased and potentially contaminates the sealed holdout: evaluating “recently promoted” strategies chosen using related evidence cannot establish prospective validity, and repeated historical probing creates multiple-comparisons risk. “Positive net EV” in each split is unmeasurable without predeclared sample-size, confidence/credible intervals, variance, turnover, and minimum economic margin thresholds; tiny or noisy splits could pass by chance or fail harmlessly. The falsification rule is underpowered—“all pass” does not show the pooled gate is safe—and it ignores regime weighting, missing data, and whether spread/slippage assumptions remain realistic, though no direct execution-safety violation is specified.
