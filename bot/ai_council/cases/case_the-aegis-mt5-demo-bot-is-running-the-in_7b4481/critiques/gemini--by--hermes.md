The central flaw is circular censoring: winners' MAE is measured under the existing 30-pip stop, so the distribution is truncated by the very parameter being replaced — any trade whose excursion exceeded 30 pips was already killed and can never appear in the winner sample, biasing the derived threshold low and guaranteeing the candidate manufactures extra stop-outs when replayed. The 95th percentile of a presumably small, single-regime winner set is a high-variance estimate with no confidence interval, no walk-forward/out-of-sample protocol, and no pre-registered numeric pass/fail criteria for "PF gain vs win-rate decay," making the headline claim unmeasurable as written. Cost interaction is ignored: tighter stops mechanically raise stop-out frequency and therefore spread/slippage drag, so the Profit Factor comparison is meaningless unless evaluated net of costs at the runner's assumptions. Finally, replacing a stop formula alters live risk geometry and must go through the governed promotion gates with ExperimentRegistry recording and operator authorization — not ship as an inline calibration tweak.
