The retrospective comparison is likely underpowered: few historical promotions and correlated market regimes make Fisher p-values and nominal CI coverage unreliable. Each candidate’s own horizon and non-overlapping block definition can be tuned post hoc, creating a new selection bias unless fixed prospectively and tested across regimes. “Realized post-promotion expectancy” is confounded by changing execution, portfolio constraints, and selection rules, so it cannot isolate whether the CI gate caused better outcomes.
