The proposal burns the sealed holdout as its only decision set: any accept/reject call made directly against the seal — especially across a stream of such challengers — is uncorrected multiple testing, so "improvement" is as likely noise as edge, and the seal is spent after one look. It specifies no minimum sample size, no significance or bootstrap testing on net expectancy or max drawdown (both extremely noisy point estimates at typical holdout trade counts), and leaves "validated-state observation" undefined, so the challenger isn't reproducible as stated. The trade-count and invalidation-rate comparisons are confounded by construction: requiring two consecutive observations mechanically reduces entries, so a large invalidation-rate shift may reflect selection rather than quality, and the 20%-count gate interacts ambiguously with the compound rejection clause ("worsening drawdown OR count reduction" vs. joint conditions is not disambiguated). There is no pre-holdout validation stage (in-sample or cross-validated screening) to justify touching the seal at all, and no statement of per-candidate horizon/overlap handling despite that being a known trap in this pipeline.
