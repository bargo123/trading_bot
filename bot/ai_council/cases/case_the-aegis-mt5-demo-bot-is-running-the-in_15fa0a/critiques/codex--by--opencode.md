The 0.10R acceptance threshold carries no significance test or confidence interval, so on a reduced sample (up to 25% fewer trades) it can easily be selection noise—especially given repeated challenger evaluations against the same sealed holdout with no multiple-comparison correction, which is itself a slow leak of the "sealed" data. Key terms are unmeasurable: "validated-state observations" is undefined (trades vs. bars), and "worsening maximum drawdown" has no quantitative tolerance. Validation is incomplete: a single holdout pass without walk-forward re-testing or sensitivity analysis around the arbitrary n=30 cutoff cannot distinguish signal from threshold-fitting, and it's unclear whether per-trade cost assumptions remain valid under the sparser trade distribution. Finally, "accept it" is ambiguous governance language—an accepted challenger must still route through every gate in `promote.py`; passing these criteria alone must never be read as promotion authority.
