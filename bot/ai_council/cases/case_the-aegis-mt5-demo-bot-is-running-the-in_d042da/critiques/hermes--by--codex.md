Rolling revalidation introduces a high overfitting risk: choosing windows by candidate horizon and tuning against sealed-slice outcomes can adapt to the holdout, especially without a pre-registered schedule, untouched final holdout, and multiple-comparison correction. The claim that rejections reflect short-lived edge is unmeasurable from rejected candidates alone, and the proposal does not define minimum sample sizes, statistical uncertainty, regime coverage, or how overlapping horizons and costs are handled. “Equal-or-lower drawdown” is insufficient for tail-risk control, while acceptance rate and invalidation rates can improve through excessive abstention; no explicit requirement preserves all existing promotion, broker, and champion-governance gates.
