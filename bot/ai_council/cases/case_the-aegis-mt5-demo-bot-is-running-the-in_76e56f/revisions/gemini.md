I propose an isolated Research Factory experiment to evaluate a "Volatility-Adjusted Slippage Threshold" (VAST) that filters firehose signals when M1 ATR exceeds the 20-day mean by two standard deviations. This study will operationalize execution degradation by measuring actual slippage against rolling average spreads, utilizing regime-stratified holdout sets and Monte Carlo simulations to mitigate overfitting risks. We will prioritize identifying a statistically significant (p < 0.05) correlation between ATR spikes and execution costs, replacing arbitrary profit targets with objective slippage-to-spread ratios. The hypothesis is falsified if VAST fails to provide a measurable reduction in execution-sensitive trades without degrading the overall expectancy across diverse market regimes. By confining this to the research-only path, we ensure that validated-state gates remain immutable until the causal link between volatility-driven slippage and firehose efficiency is empirically established.
