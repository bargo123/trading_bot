The proposal’s reliance on "estimated" slippage is a critical failure point, as these metrics rarely reflect real-market latency and order-book thinness during high-volatility events. By mandating an arbitrary 10% expectancy boost without defining a minimum sample size, you risk promoting high-variance noise that appears superior in holdouts but collapses under live execution. Additionally, the lack of a walk-forward stress test for shifting liquidity regimes makes this "sealed" experiment a recipe for overfitting to static historical spreads.
