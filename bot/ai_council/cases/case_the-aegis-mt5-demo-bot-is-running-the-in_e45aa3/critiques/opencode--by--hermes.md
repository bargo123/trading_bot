The 80th-percentile cutoff, trailing-window length, and 70% trade-count floor are three free parameters with no stated sensitivity sweep or out-of-sample confirmation — jointly tuning them against the same sealed set used for acceptance is textbook in-sample selection, and it also burns the seal: sealed holdouts get one unbiased evaluation, not iterative refinement. "Net expectancy improves" is unmeasurable as written — no minimum effect size, no significance testing or confidence interval (with ~30% fewer trades, variance alone can lift per-trade expectancy), so accept-on-any-positive-delta is a noise-magnifying rule. Validation gaps: no proof the trailing percentile is computed strictly causally at decision time (spread-series lookahead silently inflates results), no evidence reconstructed historical spreads match what the runner actually pays versus the recorded slippage assumptions, no forward/paper confirmation period before touching the champion, and no statement of how the filter stacks with the existing spread/economics gates (double-counting or gate-ordering effects unaddressed). Safety-wise it's largely clean — read-only evaluation, champion frozen absent full gate passage, within the governed promotion path — but it must be logged in ExperimentRegistry like any other experiment, and "DEMO-equivalent" needs a precise definition of how the equivalence was constructed rather than asserted.
