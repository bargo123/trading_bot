**Proposal (rev. 2) — reconcile the `manual`-exit bucket before any new signal work.** The reconciled-exit evidence (`outcome_learning.json`, n=2,518) shows 76% of exits labeled `manual` (n=1,928, expectancy −0.013) versus positive `tp` (n=447, +0.107) and rare but ruinous `sl` (n=96, −0.547) exits, so we test whether the aggregate −0.012 expectancy reflects exit mis-attribution rather than entry quality. One registered experiment joins each `manual`-closed demo ticket to MT5 deal history and the runner's intent log via the predeclared key (ticket ID → deal position ID, exact match only), with a predeclared coverage gate: if fewer than 90% of manual tickets resolve to a definitive cause, the outcome is recorded as inconclusive and no inference is drawn. Ambiguity handling is fixed in advance — conflicting evidence defaults conservatively back to `manual`, and a blind-audited sample of 100 tickets provides ground-truth validation requiring agreement κ ≥ 0.7 before any classification results are reported. We accept that relabeling cannot change realized expectancy: the primary outcome is corrected per-cause attribution with binomial confidence intervals on the reclassified share, the hypothesis ("most manual labels are bot-initiated time/max-age exits") is registered before data access, and no sign-flip or percentage threshold is asserted. A finding of genuinely external closes generates only a stop-placement hypothesis; any resulting challenger must separately clear every gate in `promote.py`, net of costs, on the sealed holdout before champion consideration.
