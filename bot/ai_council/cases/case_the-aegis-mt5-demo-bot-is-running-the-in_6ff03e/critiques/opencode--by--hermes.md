The acceptance thresholds are unmeasurable as stated — "improves out-of-sample expectancy" and "preserving or reducing maximum drawdown" carry no minimum margin, confidence interval, or statistical significance requirement, and with no per-session trade-count floor a noise-level edge on a handful of delayed entries passes. Reusing the same "sealed historical sessions" for yet another candidate is holdout erosion: after many challengers have been scored against that partition, selecting whichever survives is multiple-testing overfitting, so the design needs either a freshly sealed split or an explicit multiple-comparison correction. The rule itself is operationally underspecified — which state classifier, what counts as "consistent," over what lookback and evaluated at which bar timestamps — and since forcing two consecutive classifications delays entry into different spread/slippage regimes, the cost comparison must be re-estimated for the shifted fill distribution rather than reusing the current rule's assumptions. It also omits ExperimentRegistry registration, violating the lab's every-experiment-recorded gate before any backtest runs.
