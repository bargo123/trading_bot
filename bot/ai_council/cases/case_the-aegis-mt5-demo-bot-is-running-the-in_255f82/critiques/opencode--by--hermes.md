The proposal's acceptance gate never tests its own claim: "false starts" is undefined and never measured, so even a pass on expectancy/count wouldn't validate the hypothesized mechanism. Both acceptance thresholds ("net expectancy improves," ≥70% of baseline count) are arbitrary and unregistered, with no significance test, minimum sample size, or confidence intervals — a ~30% trade reduction easily produces a spurious expectancy bump, and if the two-confirmation lag was chosen after looking at this same sealed data, multiple comparisons have already burned the holdout. "Validated-state confirmations" is not operationally defined, and aggregate expectancy comparisons are confounded unless each candidate signal is paired timestamp-by-timestamp against the baseline's trades on identical windows. Finally, replay-based costs understate the real problem: delaying entry to a second confirmation systematically fills at different (likely wider) spread regimes than the baseline assumed, and per governance the run must be registered in ExperimentRegistry with the sealed holdout provably untouched before this single evaluation — anything less is an automatic process rejection regardless of results.
