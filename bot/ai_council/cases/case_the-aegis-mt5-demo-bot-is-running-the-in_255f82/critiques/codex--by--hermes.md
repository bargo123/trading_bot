The promotion criterion is overfitting-prone and under-specified: "invalidation-adjusted drawdown" and "execution-cost-adjusted returns" have no pre-registered formulas or weights, so the threshold can be silently tuned until the challenger wins, and adjudicating on a single "next" sealed holdout is a one-draw experiment with no multiple-testing control if the comparison is ever rerun. The A/B is confounded by selection — the stricter gate enters later on a different subset of opportunities, so higher expectancy may simply reflect skipping marginal entries while the smaller trade count inflates variance, yet the design demands no bootstrap CIs, significance test, power analysis, or second-regime replication to distinguish that from real improvement. Several claims are unmeasurable as written: "timestamp-consistent" lacks a tolerance window and data-source definition, and "fill quality" is undefined (spread-at-fill vs. mid, measured on which DEMO fills). Governance is incomplete: no ExperimentRegistry registration or explicit routing through `promote.py` gates / `research_promote_champion.py` is stated, and the added pre-entry delay is itself an execution-behavior change whose latency cost goes unmeasured before promotion.
