The 60-second/three-scan dwell parameters are unjustified magic numbers with no stated sensitivity analysis, and evaluating a single hand-picked configuration against a sealed holdout invites repeat queries until one passes — there's no pre-registered effect size, significance test, or bootstrap CI, which matters enormously because the filter mechanically slashes trade count and leaves expectancy differences noise-dominated. Worse, validating "remained valid across three consecutive 20-second scans" historically is only legitimate if replayed from actually recorded scan-level state history; reconstructing validity from bars or outcome logs would be silent lookahead. The proposal also measures maximum adverse excursion but excludes it from the acceptance rule — an unbound metric that can be selectively reported post hoc — and never addresses whether reduced participation during fast regimes changes tail exposure in ways net expectancy alone won't show. Compliance-wise it stays inside research bounds (registry, DEMO assumptions, no order placement), but the acceptance gate as written would let a statistically meaningless improvement promote.
