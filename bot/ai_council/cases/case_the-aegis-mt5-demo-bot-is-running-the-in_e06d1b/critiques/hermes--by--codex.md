The counterfactual outcomes of rejected signals are not necessarily observable or executable, so paper-path economics can understate market impact, latency, adverse selection, and downstream gate effects; this is a biased selected population, not a clean measure of discarded edge. Four weeks may provide too few independent trades for a bootstrap lower bound, especially with serially correlated signals, while “one average spread unit” and “significantly positive” are undefined and invite post hoc thresholds. Conditioning a later gate recalibration on these results and recently added features risks data snooping or holdout leakage unless the hypothesis, threshold, features, and untouched evaluation set are pre-registered; the proposal also lacks power analysis and a direct live-vs-paper validation of the counterfactual simulator.
