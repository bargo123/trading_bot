Revised proposal: quantify conservatism cost in the validated-state gate by logging every rejected signal with its counterfactual outcome under a pre-registered simulator that explicitly models fillability (spread-crossing versus queue assumptions), execution latency, concurrent-position limits, and overlap deduplication among clustered signals, priced net of the runner's measured spread/slippage distribution aggregated per pre-specified session buckets. The primary endpoint replaces "one average spread unit" with a pre-registered threshold: the mean counterfactual EV per independent trade exceeds zero with a 95% lower bound, where independence is enforced by block-clustered bootstrap over time-regime strata and serially correlated signal clusters. Collection runs in four-week blocks and extends in two-week increments until a minimum of ~200 independent post-deduplication rejected signals is reached, sized by an ex-ante power calculation for the one-sided test at the estimated intra-cluster correlation. Because counterfactuals are non-executable and the rejected population is selected, simulator validity is tested read-only by replaying gate-passed signals through the same path and comparing predictions against the governed runner's recorded DEMO fills (research makes no MT5 calls), with a pre-set calibration tolerance whose violation voids inference. Hypothesis, thresholds, features, and evaluation split are registered in ExperimentRegistry before data accrual; only the training window is analyzed, the sealed holdout stays untouched, and any gate recalibration using the new short-horizon session features proceeds solely as a separate pre-registered gated experiment judged on that holdout.
